How much is learned, and what are the limits?
  1. Unsupervised atoms. Each patch is reduced to 4×4 RGB averages. Similar appearances form an online competitive codebook, and each codeword retains the average of its full-resolution 16×16 training pixels for generation. This is a readable patch dictionary, not a deep neural network.
  2. Self-discovered local relationships. From complete training images, every spatial site tries all pairs of its eight neighbor offsets. It accumulates conditional counts and scores relations by their predictive lift over the frequency of the target atom, discounted by support. No privileged eye/eye/mouth offsets or product feature are inserted into the learner.
  3. Completion. Visible patches are encoded once; hidden patches are replaced with learned codewords chosen using visible context, with the strongest relationships filled first. Predicted pixels come from the learned prototypes—not from the hidden sensor image or from an original tile supplied at test time.
  4. Limits. This synthetic world deliberately reuses a small alphabet of 16×16 glyphs. Discovering an association here does not imply learning natural-image semantics. This model is still single-scale, local and discrete. Half-scene completions can be wrong or generic, and it has no mechanism yet for generating previously unseen detailed textures.
  5. Controls. Two-input versus one-input inference on exactly the same learned observations; opposite worlds with identical individual and pairwise statistics; erased learners; held-out unseen seeds; a masked-pixel nonleakage test in CI. The geometry search has a fixed neighborhood radius, but the successful offset pair is selected from data.
